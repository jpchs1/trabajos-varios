Our recommendations & itinerary adjustments

Based on your original request, we have made a small number of adjustments — to the start date, to where you sleep, and to which boat you take — in order to give you a calmer travel pace and safer flight connections. Nothing below is a decision we have taken for you. Each one is set out with what you asked for, what we propose instead, and why; tell us which you want and we will re-issue.

1Start on 14 November — and arrive in Santiago on the 21st, not the 22nd
You askedThe tour can start on 14 or 15 November — please advise. And: “I need to be in Santiago on the 22nd Nov.”
We proposeStart on 14 November. The programme ends with your arrival in Santiago on the evening of Saturday 21 November, leaving Sunday the 22nd entirely clear.
WhyUshuaia and El Calafate are two of the most weather-exposed airports in Argentina, and November is the windiest month of the Patagonian spring. Same-day cancellations and re-timings happen. If you start on the 15th, the only way the numbers work is to fly Ushuaia → Buenos Aires → Santiago on the 22nd itself — so a single delayed leg on your last morning would break the one date you cannot move.
BenefitA full spare day protecting your fixed date; a night's rest in Santiago before it; and, because the extra day sits inside the trip rather than at the end of it, a third night in Ushuaia instead of two.
Trade-offOne additional night's accommodation. In our view it is the best-value night in the whole programme.
Safer air connectionsLower operational riskMore time per destination
2Sleep two nights in El Chaltén rather than day-tripping from El Calafate
You askedEl Calafate, El Chaltén and Ushuaia, with the most scenic areas around El Chaltén and Mt. Fitz Roy — easy walks of 1 to 2 hours.
We propose2 nights El Calafate · 2 nights El Chaltén · 3 nights Ushuaia. El Chaltén becomes a stay, not an excursion.
WhyEl Chaltén is three hours each way from El Calafate. The standard day trip leaves at 06:30 and returns after 21:00 — thirteen to fourteen hours, six of them seated in a vehicle, with the walking squeezed into the middle of the day when the light is at its flattest. Staying in the village turns one punishing day into two unhurried half-days.
BenefitNo pre-dawn start. Fitz Roy from your window at first light and again at dusk — the two hours photographers actually travel for. And the walks are done at your speed, with the option to turn back at any point without losing the day.
Trade-offTwo hotel changes instead of one. We have kept every other base fixed to compensate — you unpack four times in eight days, which is as few as this route allows.

3The one-hour glacier boat, not the nine-hour one
You asked“The Perito Moreno Glacier, the boat trip.”

4Fly the exit day on one airline, through one Buenos Aires airport
You askedDomestic flights included, and to be in Santiago for the 22nd.
We proposeUshuaia → Buenos Aires Aeroparque (AEP) → Santiago, both legs on Aerolíneas Argentinas, on one ticket.
WhyThe obvious alternatives are worse in ways that are not obvious until the day. Most routings land you at Aeroparque and depart from Ezeiza — a 45-to-70-minute road transfer across the city with your luggage, plus a full re-check. Others split the day between two airlines, which means that if the Ushuaia leg is late, the second carrier owes you nothing.
BenefitNo airport change, no taxi across Buenos Aires, no re-checking bags — and if the southbound leg runs late, the airline is obliged to re-book you onto the next Santiago service.
Trade-offIt is still a long travel day, roughly nine hours door to door. If you would rather break it, we can insert a night in Buenos Aires on the 20th — see option below. Ushuaia would then drop back to two nights.

5Isla Martillo: yes — and it has to be held early
You asked“Visiting Martillo Island for the penguins (if possible).”
We proposeYes — and specifically the landing version, via Estancia Harberton, held the moment you confirm rather than nearer the date.
WhyTwo products share the name. Most Beagle Channel boats only sail past Isla Martillo; a single licensed operator is permitted to land, and the number of visitors allowed ashore each day is capped by the provincial authority. In the November–December colony season those places are typically gone months ahead. Given that you have already done the Beagle Channel, the sail-past version would add nothing.
BenefitYou walk a marked path along the beach with Magellanic penguins either side of you — the colony is at its noisiest in late November — rather than photographing them from fifty metres of water.
Trade-offNinety minutes each way, the last stretch on gravel road. The walking itself is flat, slow and about an hour. If the landing permit cannot be secured we will tell you before you pay a peso, and refund that line in full.

7How we sequence Rapa Nui — because two of its sites admit you only once
You askedShared guided excursions covering the main archaeological and cultural highlights of Rapa Nui.
We proposeThree circuits, one per day, never two: the eastern full day (Rano Raraku and Tongariki), the southern morning (Rano Kau and Orongo), the western morning (Puna Pau, Ahu Akivi and the caves) — with both afternoons after the half-days left free.
WhyYour national park ticket admits you once to Rano Raraku and once to Orongo, and the ranger scans it at the gate. Itineraries that “pass by” either site on more than one day quietly burn an entry you cannot buy back. Sequencing them into single, unhurried visits is not a stylistic choice — it is the only way to get the full value of the ticket.
BenefitNo wasted entries, and real time at Rano Raraku — the quarry where four hundred moai still lie half-carved in the hillside, and the one place everyone wishes they had been given another hour. Two free afternoons in Hanga Roa for the museum, the market and a slow lunch.

Vegetarian, in two countries that are not

  • We tell every hotel and every excursion at the time of booking, not on the day. On the Lago del Desierto excursion and at the Rapa Nui dinner, the menu is fixed and set in advance — those are the two that must be flagged early, and we do.

  • Hanga Roa needs a little more care. The island's food is fish-led; the hotel breakfast is well stocked, and we will name the three restaurants that do this properly.
  • Tell us about eggs, dairy and onion/garlic and we will pass that on precisely. It is a different brief in each case and we would rather ask than guess.

Things to know
